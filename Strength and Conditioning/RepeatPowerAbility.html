Mastering Repeat Power Ability (RPA)

A technical analysis of Alex Natera’s framework utilizing High Volume Power Training (HVPT) to build unparalleled neuromuscular fatigue resistance in fast-twitch fibers.

RPA vs. Traditional RSA

Understanding the fundamental physiological distinction between metabolic conditioning and neuromuscular power endurance. This section clarifies why traditional sprint conditioning fails to maintain explosive actions in late-game scenarios.

Repeated Sprint Ability (RSA)

Traditionally focuses on maintaining running speed over repeated efforts (e.g., 6 x 30m sprints). It heavily taxes the cardiovascular and metabolic systems, often resulting in a shift toward slow-twitch fiber dominance to manage metabolic byproducts.

Metric: Speed/Time decrement.

Repeat Power Ability (RPA)

Focuses on the neuromuscular system's ability to resist fatigue in fast-twitch (Type II) fibers. The goal is to sustain peak force generation and rate of force development (RFD) across repeated explosive actions (jumps, tackles, rapid accelerations) without shifting muscle recruitment strategies.

Metric: Peak Power/Force decrement.

The LCMJ20 Assessment

The Loaded Countermovement Jump 20 protocol is the gold standard for quantifying an athlete's RPA. Explore how the Percent Decrement Score is calculated to identify neuromuscular drop-off.

Protocol Parameters

  • Load: Optimal Power Load (often 20-40% 1RM or specific bodyweight multiples via hex bar).
  • Volume: 20 maximal effort countermovement jumps.
  • Pacing: Continuous or strictly clustered (e.g., every 5 seconds).
  • Metric: Peak Power (W) or Jump Height (cm) per repetition.

Percent Decrement Score (S&d)

Calculates the drop-off in performance relative to the athlete's ideal theoretical performance (their best jump multiplied by 20).

S&d = 100 - ((Total Height / (Best Height * 20)) * 100)
Calculated Decrement Score --% Lower score indicates superior fast-twitch fatigue resistance.

HVPT 4-Week Progression

High Volume Power Training (HVPT) is the driver for RPA adaptation. Utilizing submaximal loads (30-40% 1RM) with maximum intent, this protocol manipulating volume and rest intervals forces fast-twitch fibers to become fatigue-resistant.

Load 30-35% 1RM
Sets x Reps 4 x 5
Rest Interval 2.5 - 3 min
Primary Objective Base Mechanical Efficiency

Application Notes: The initial week focuses on establishing perfect mechanics with the chosen implement (e.g., hex bar jump squats). The generous rest intervals ensure each rep is performed with absolute maximal intent. Power decrement within the set should be minimal.

Load 35-40% 1RM
Sets x Reps 5 x 5
Rest Interval 2.0 min
Primary Objective Increasing Density

Application Notes: Volume is slightly increased, and rest is tightened. This begins to selectively stress the neuromuscular system's recovery capacity between sets. If velocity drops by more than 10-15% within a set, the load or reps must be adjusted to maintain the *power* stimulus.

Load 40% 1RM
Sets x Reps 6 x 4
Rest Interval 90 sec
Primary Objective Maximum Neurological Demand

Application Notes: The peak of the microcycle. Reps per set are slightly reduced to ensure peak force on every effort, but sets and density are maxed out. This is where the true fast-twitch fatigue resistance adaptations are forced. Extreme focus on intention is required.

Load 30% 1RM
Sets x Reps 3 x 3
Rest Interval 3.0 min
Primary Objective Taper & Realization

Application Notes: Significant reduction in volume and an increase in rest. The goal here is to shed the systemic fatigue accumulated in weeks 1-3, allowing the newly adapted fast-twitch endurance to be expressed as peak Repeat Power Ability on the field.
