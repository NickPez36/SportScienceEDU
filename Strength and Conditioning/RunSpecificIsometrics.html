Run-Specific Isometric Training (RSIST)

An interactive high-performance coaching manual based on the methodologies of Alex Natera. Explore the physiological nuances, biomechanical specificities, and practical microdosing strategies to enhance Rate of Force Development (RFD) and tendon stiffness for sprint performance.

1. Action Typology: PIMA vs. HIMA

Understanding the intent behind the isometric contraction is critical. While no gross joint movement occurs in either, the neuromuscular mapping and resulting physiological adaptations differ significantly. Select an action below to compare.

The Intent

Attempting to push or overcome an immovable resistance (e.g., pushing a barbell hard into safety pins). The intent is concentric.

Physiological Impact

  • Rapid motor unit recruitment (early phase neural drive).
  • Superior for enhancing Rate of Force Development (RFD).
  • Slight fascicle shortening occurs as the tendon lengthens (series elastic component stretch).
  • Faster relaxation rates post-contraction.

The Intent

Attempting to resist a load from moving you, preventing yielding (e.g., holding a heavy barbell steady in a lunge position). The intent is anti-eccentric.

Physiological Impact

  • Maintains sustained motor unit firing.
  • Superior for increasing tendon stiffness and tissue robustness.
  • Reduces cortical inhibition (golgi tendon organ down-regulation).
  • Creates greater structural muscle damage if taken to failure (due to micro-yielding).

2. Kinematic Specificity: The Stance Phase

Isometrics are highly joint-angle specific (adaptations occur ±15 degrees from the training angle). RSIST specifically targets the biomechanics of the maximal velocity sprint stance phase to ensure transfer of training.

HIP
~150°

Slight Extension

Mimics the upright posture of max velocity. Requires massive gluteal and proximal hamstring isometric force to maintain pelvic stability against ground reaction forces.

KNEE
~140°

Shallow Flexion

The critical 'amortization' angle. Training here builds the stiffness required to prevent knee collapse during ground contact, minimizing ground contact time.

ANKLE
~90°

Neutral Dorsiflexion

The Achilles must act as a rigid spring. Isometric training at neutral ensures the calf complex can handle massive forces without yielding into excessive dorsiflexion.

3. The RSIST Hierarchy

Athletes must earn the right to progress. Moving to dynamic isometrics before establishing foundational stiffness leads to energy leaks and injury risk. Click to expand each phase.

Goal: Establish structural tolerance, tendon stiffness, and postural capacity.

Action: Holding heavy loads (barbell or heavy bands) at specific sprint angles, fighting gravity to prevent yielding. Long durations (3-10 seconds) to build time-under-tension.

Goal: Maximize Rate of Force Development (RFD) and peak force generation.

Action: Pushing maximally against an immovable object (e.g., barbell set under safety pins). Short durations (2-3 seconds) focusing entirely on the speed and intent of force application.

Goal: Inter-muscular coordination, rapid contraction/relaxation cycling.

Action: Dynamic. Starting in an Iso-Hold (e.g., split stance), rapidly switching leg positions in the air, and instantly stabilizing back into the target isometric joint angles upon landing. High neural demand.

Goal: Extreme eccentric-to-isometric force attenuation; extreme tendon stiffness demand.

Action: Dynamic. A free-falling mass (barbell, or athlete's own bodyweight dropping from a box) is caught and arrested instantly at the specific joint angle. Mimics the brutal ground reaction forces of maximal sprinting.

5. Loading Parameters & Microdosing

RSIST can be programmed as a primary central nervous system (CNS) stimulus in the gym, or 'microdosed' track-side to potentiate the neuromuscular system just prior to sprinting.

Parameter Guideline Rationale
Action Type Iso-Push (PIMA) or low-volume Iso-Switch Maximize neural drive (Post-Activation Potentiation) without inducing fatigue.
Intensity 95-100% Intent (Fast push) Intent dictates early phase RFD recruitment.
Duration 1.5 - 3 seconds max Keep it short. Beyond 3s causes unnecessary metabolic buildup.
Volume 2-3 sets of 2-3 reps/leg Microdose. Just enough to prime the system.
Equipment Bodyweight against partner, immovable fence, or heavy band. Must be practical on the track without heavy hardware.
Parameter Guideline Rationale
Action Type Heavy Iso-Hold (HIMA) or Iso-Catch Create structural change, tendon stiffness, and absolute force capacity.
Intensity 80-100% MVC or 150-250%+ Bodyweight Requires massive systemic load to force adaptation in stiff tissues (tendons).
Duration 3 - 6 seconds (Holds); Instantaneous (Catches) Longer time under high tension for tissue architectual changes.
Volume 3-5 sets of 3-5 reps/leg Primary session volume. Expect CNS fatigue post-session.
Equipment Power rack, heavy barbell, safety pins, force plates (ideal). Controlled environment to safely apply and measure supramaximal loads.
