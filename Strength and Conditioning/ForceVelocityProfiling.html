The Roadmap of Human Performance

Bridging the gap between neuromuscular physiology, biomechanics, and applied strength & conditioning. While RSI, DSI, and RFD dictate force production, Force-Velocity (FVP) and Load-Velocity (LVP) Profiling prescribe the exact physiological adaptations required.

⚙ 1. Neurophysiological Mechanisms

This section dissects the conceptual differences between macroscopic mechanical capabilities mapping (FVP) and external mass manipulation for motor unit recruitment (LVP).

Force-Velocity Profiling (FVP)

Maps the macroscopic mechanical capabilities of the neuromuscular system during multi-joint movements (e.g., squat jump, sprint acceleration). Based on the work of J.B. Morin and P. Samozino.

  • F₀ (Theoretical Max Force): The y-intercept; maximum force capability at zero velocity.
  • v₀ (Theoretical Max Velocity): The x-intercept; maximum velocity capability at zero resistance.
  • Sfv (Slope of F-V curve): Indicates the individual's ratio of force to velocity qualities.
  • Optimal Profile: The specific Sfv that maximizes ballistic performance (e.g., jump height) for an individual's given power output.

Load-Velocity Profiling (LVP)

Evaluates the relationship between external mass (load) and movement velocity during traditional strength exercises. Serves as the foundation for Velocity-Based Training (VBT).

  • Physiological Basis: Maps directly to the Size Principle of motor unit recruitment.
  • Intent to Move: Relies entirely on compensatory acceleration—the athlete must attempt to move the load as fast as possible regardless of mass.
  • Linearity: Exhibits a highly stable linear relationship across submaximal loads up to the 1RM threshold.
  • Autoregulation: Used daily to adjust load based on readiness, bypassing subjective 1RM percentages.

📏 2. Measurement & Quantification

This section outlines the gold-standard testing protocols to derive both profiles, detailing instrumentation and analytical models.

Jumping Force-Velocity Profiling

Determines the optimal loading parameters for vertical ballistic tasks. Often tested via Squat Jumps (SJ) to isolate concentric capability.

  • Protocol: Multiple loaded jumps (e.g., Bodyweight, 20%, 40%, 60% of BM).
  • Instrumentation: Force plates (gold standard) or validated contact mats/optical systems using flight-time derived equations (Samozino's method).
  • Output: Identifies specific Force Deficits or Velocity Deficits relative to an athlete's theoretical optimum curve.
Sfv = -F₀ / v₀

Sprinting Force-Velocity Profiling

Maps the macroscopic F-V relationship specifically during the acceleration phase of a sprint.

  • Protocol: Single maximal 30m or 40m sprint from a split stance.
  • Instrumentation: Radar guns, laser devices, motorized resistance devices (e.g., 1080 Sprint), or accurately spaced timing gates mapped against anthropometrics.
  • Key Metric: Ratio of Forces (RF). Highlights the proportion of total force directed horizontally. Decline in RF limits top speed more than total force production.

LVP & Submaximal 1RM Prediction

Utilizes incremental load testing to establish an individualized L-V linear regression, enabling daily autoregulation and 1RM prediction without maximal, fatiguing lifts.

  • Protocol: 3 to 4 submaximal incremental loads (e.g., 40%, 60%, 80% estimated 1RM).
  • Instrumentation: Linear Position Transducers (LPTs) or validated accelerometers.
  • Key Variable: Mean Propulsive Velocity (MPV).
  • Prediction: The linear regression line is extrapolated to the Minimum Velocity Threshold (MVT) of a specific exercise (e.g., ~0.30 m/s for Bench Press) to predict true 1RM.

🏃 3. Practical Uses by Athlete Typology

Click on an athletic discipline to reveal how high-performance directors apply these models to manipulate biological output and resolve sport-specific constraints.

Endurance Profile & Application

Primary Tool: Load-Velocity Profiling (VBT)

For concurrent training models, neuromuscular fatigue management is paramount. Endurance athletes utilize LVP to maintain maximum force output capabilities while strictly minimizing Central Nervous System (CNS) fatigue and mechanical muscle damage.

  • Strict velocity loss cutoffs (e.g., ending sets at 10% velocity drop) prevents hypertrophy pathways and DOMS.
  • Ensures strength sessions do not impair aerobic pathway adaptations or scheduled track volumes.

Middle-Distance Profile & Application

Primary Tool: Sprinting FVP & LVP Hybrid

Sprint FVP is leveraged to identify mechanical inefficiencies during acceleration, which deeply impacts running economy. Optimizing F₀ improves ground reaction force mechanics, while elevating v₀ provides the mechanical reserve necessary for a devastating finishing kick.

  • Correcting Force Deficits improves overall stride length efficiency at submaximal paces.
  • LVP is used to schedule explosive strength work without overloading the glycolytic system.

Sprinters & Power Profile & Application

Primary Tool: Jumping & Sprinting FVP

FVP is the definitive diagnostic tool. It isolates exactly whether a power athlete suffers from a "Force Deficit" (unable to produce massive force rapidly) or a "Velocity Deficit" (unable to continue producing force at high movement speeds). The application of horizontal force (Ratio of Forces, RF) distinguishes elite acceleration from average.

  • Directs programming bias: If F₀ dominant but low speed, prescribe assisted sprinting. If high speed but low F₀, prescribe heavy sleds.
  • Monitors the technical capability to orient force horizontally, independent of total force capacity.

Applying FVP: Deficit Correction

The objective is shifting the F-V curve toward the athlete's theoretical optimum based on their specific deficit.

Force Deficit ▼

Prescription: High Load, Low Velocity

Athlete lacks baseline neuromuscular output. Exercises must increase F₀.

  • Deep Squats (>85% 1RM)
  • Heavy Sled Pushing/Tows (>50% bodyweight resistance)
  • Overcoming Isometrics
Velocity Deficit ▼

Prescription: Low Load, High Velocity

Athlete is strong but cannot express force rapidly. Exercises must increase v₀.

  • Assisted Sprinting (Bungee/1080 Sprint overspeed)
  • Overspeed Jumps / Band-accelerated jumps
  • Shock Method / High-intensity plyometrics

Applying LVP: Velocity-Based Zones

Using real-time device feedback (MPV) to dictate daily autoregulation and manage neuromuscular fatigue.

Velocity Loss Thresholds (Fatigue Management)
10% Drop Peaking/In-Season. Stops set early. Protects CNS, max power, minimal hypertrophy.

📝 5. Summary Matrix

A structural breakdown contrasting the macro-level capabilities of FVP versus the micro-level prescription rules of LVP.

Concept Force-Velocity Profiling (FVP) Load-Velocity Profiling (LVP)
Core Purpose Map mechanical capabilities & identify systemic deficits relative to an optimal profile. Map mass-to-speed relationships for exercise prescription and fatigue monitoring.
Key Metrics F₀ (max force), v₀ (max velocity), Sfv (profile slope), Ratio of Forces (RF). Mean Propulsive Velocity (MPV), MVT (Minimum Velocity Threshold), Predicted 1RM.
Testing Methods Multiple loaded jumps (force plates), single max sprints (radar/timing gates). Incremental submaximal loads (3-4 sets) monitored via LPTs or accelerometers.
Primary Use in Programming Exercise selection (e.g., prescribing heavy sleds vs overspeed jumps to shift the curve). Daily autoregulation, defining training zones, capping volume via velocity loss thresholds.
