Anatoliy Bondarchuk’s System

02 Physical Training Resonance

Bondarchuk argues that human adaptation is not purely additive. When specific exercises from different classifications are combined within a training session or block, they can create a "Physical Training Resonance."

This resonance implies that the central nervous system integrates the localized stimuli (e.g., strength from an SPE, specific speed from an SDE) to produce a combined effect on the competitive exercise that is greater than the sum of its parts. It is the harmonious interaction of localized adaptations driving global performance.

03 Peaking Strategies: Complex vs. Sequential

Traditional periodization often utilizes a Sequential approach (block periodization), where physiological qualities (Hypertrophy, Max Strength, Power) are trained linearly in separate phases. Bondarchuk utilizes a Complex approach, developing all targeted physiological abilities simultaneously within the same training session and block, maintaining the same exercises until adaptation is exhausted.

All physiological targets (Speed, Strength, Technique) are stimulated simultaneously in every session. The athlete adapts to the entire complex of stimuli at once.

04 Monitoring the Individual Response Curve

Because Bondarchuk's complex blocks maintain the exact same exercises and structure every session, the only variable is the athlete's physiological state. By measuring the Competitive Exercise (CE) or a primary SDE every single session, the coach can plot the individual's "Response Curve." Peak form is not predicted by a calendar; it is observed in real-time. When the curve plateaus at a new high, adaptation is complete, and the exercise complex must change.

05 Long-Term Delayed Training Effect (LDTE)

What happens when the athlete reaches peak form and the training complex is changed or volume is significantly reduced? Bondarchuk noted the phenomenon of the Long-Term Delayed Training Effect.

Due to the removal of residual fatigue from the previous block, the functional capabilities developed do not disappear immediately. Instead, they often result in an even higher peak (supercompensation) occurring 2 to 4 weeks after the conclusion of the heavy developmental block. This requires precise timing relative to major competitions.
