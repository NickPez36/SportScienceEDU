Solving the "Daily 1RM" Fluctuation

Traditional percentage-based training assumes an athlete's 1-Repetition Maximum (1RM) is static. In a professional setting, factors like match fatigue, sleep, nutrition, and CNS stress cause the actual daily 1RM to fluctuate by up to 18%. Velocity-Based Training (VBT) uses real-time barbell speeds to autoregulate loads, ensuring the intended physiological stimulus matches daily readiness.

⚠ The Problem with Fixed %

If an athlete is prescribed 5x5 @ 85% based on a 1RM from 4 weeks ago, but is highly fatigued today, that 85% might actually be 95% of their Daily 1RM. This turns a speed/power session into a grinding, heavy session, further taxing an already drained CNS.

✓ The VBT Solution

By prescribing a target velocity (e.g., "Work up to a load that moves at 0.50 m/s"), the absolute weight on the bar automatically adjusts. On a fresh day, the athlete lifts more weight at 0.50 m/s. On a fatigued day, they lift less. The physiological intent remains perfectly constant.

Defining Velocity Zones

Specific movement velocities correlate tightly with distinct physiological traits along the Force-Velocity curve. By targeting specific speed ranges in meters per second (m/s), coaches can predictably develop traits from absolute strength to pure speed, regardless of the weight on the bar.

Standardized VBT Zones (m/s)

General benchmarks for core compound lifts (e.g., Back Squat). Hover for details.

<

Lead-Strength (Absolute Strength) • < 0.50 m/s

Maximum force output. Grinding reps used to increase the athlete's raw ceiling. High CNS demand. Typically 85-100% 1RM equivalents.

~

Strength-Speed • 0.50 - 0.75 m/s

Moving relatively heavy loads as fast as possible. Peak power optimization for stronger, larger athletes (e.g., Linemen, Forwards).

~

Speed-Strength • 0.75 - 1.00 m/s

Moving moderate loads highly explosively. Peak power optimization for speed/skill players (e.g., Wide Receivers, Guards).

~

Starting Strength • 1.00 - 1.30 m/s

Overcoming inertia rapidly with light loads. Often involves ballistic variations (e.g., jumps with an empty bar or light DBs).

>

Reactive Strength • > 1.30 m/s

Unloaded/very light plyometrics, bounding, and sprinting. Maximizing stretch-shortening cycle (SSC) efficiency and ground contact times.

Creating Load-Velocity Profiles (LVP)

Every athlete has a unique physiological signature. A Load-Velocity Profile maps an individual's specific speed at varying percentages of their 1RM. This allows us to establish an athlete's linear regression equation, generating highly accurate, customized training prescriptions without ever testing a true 1-Rep Max.

The Profiling Process (4-Step Protocol)

  1. Warm-Up & Setup: Standardize the warm-up. Attach the linear position transducer (VBT unit) to the barbell.
  2. Sub-Maximal Data Collection: The athlete performs 1-2 reps at maximal intentional velocity at four distinct incremental loads (e.g., approx 40%, 60%, 75%, and 85% of estimated 1RM).
  3. Plot the Data: Record the Mean Concentric Velocity (MCV) for the fastest rep at each load. Plot Velocity (y-axis) against Load (x-axis).
  4. Determine MVT & Extrapolate: Apply a linear trendline. Identify the athlete's Minimal Velocity Threshold (MVT)—the speed of their 1RM (usually ~0.30 m/s for squats). The software extrapolates the trendline to the MVT to calculate the daily 1RM precisely.

Intra-Session Fatigue Management

In-season professional sports require extreme CNS management. VBT introduces Velocity Loss Thresholds. Instead of prescribing a fixed number of reps (e.g., 4x8), coaches prescribe a starting load/velocity and cut the set once velocity drops by a specific percentage. This objectively manages metabolic fatigue and recovery times.

Rep-by-Rep Velocity Drop-Off

Observe how sets are auto-terminated based on the chosen physiological goal.

10% Loss: Power & CNS Maintenance

Goal: Maintain fast-twitch fiber firing rates without causing metabolic accumulation.

Application: In-season or peaking phases. The set is terminated early (e.g., after 3 reps instead of 6). This ensures the athlete leaves the gym fresh, with high neuromuscular readiness for field practice or games within 24-48 hours.

30% Loss: Hypertrophy & Work Capacity

Goal: Structural adaptation, muscular endurance, and metabolic stress.

Application: Off-season or early pre-season. Allowing reps to grind down to a 30% loss forces recruitment of higher-threshold motor units under fatigue. Causes significant delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS) and requires 48-72+ hours of CNS recovery.
