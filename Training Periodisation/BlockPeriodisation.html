The ATR Framework: Physiological Targets

Unlike traditional periodization which attempts to develop multiple abilities simultaneously, Issurin's model uses highly concentrated loads in sequenced blocks. Each block focuses on a minimal number of compatible physiological targets.

1. Accumulation

▲

Duration: 2-6 weeks

Goal: Develop basic abilities. High volume, low/moderate intensity.

  • Aerobic cardiovascular endurance
  • Basic muscular strength / Hypertrophy
  • Movement biomechanics

2. Transmutation

⚡

Duration: 2-4 weeks

Goal: Translate basic abilities into sport-specific readiness. Volume drops, intensity spikes.

  • Anaerobic/Glycolytic endurance
  • Strength endurance / Power
  • Sport-specific technical tactics

3. Realization

🎯

Duration: 1-2 weeks

Goal: Restoration and peaking for competition. Lowest volume, maximal intensity/speed.

  • Maximal speed and explosiveness
  • Event-specific readiness
  • Full neuromuscular recovery

Applying Concentrated Loads by Discipline

The duration and physiological targets of the ATR blocks are heavily modified based on the athlete's primary energy system and muscular demands. Select an athlete profile below to see how block periodization is adapted.

Endurance Athletes

Accumulation Strategy

Duration: Prolonged (4-6 weeks). Endurance athletes require extensive time to trigger morphological changes such as increased mitochondrial density, aerobic enzymes (e.g., Citrate Synthase), and capillarization. The concentrated load is overwhelmingly high-volume, low-intensity aerobic work.

Transmutation Strategy

Duration: Moderate (2-3 weeks). The focus shifts to anaerobic threshold and race-pace mechanics. Because the aerobic base (established in Accumulation) has a very long residual effect, the athlete can safely drop pure aerobic volume to focus on higher intensity threshold work without losing baseline fitness.

Realization Strategy

Duration: Short (1-2 weeks). A steep taper. Volume drops drastically to shed accumulated fatigue. Intensity remains specific to race pace to keep the neuromuscular system primed, relying on the residual effects of the previous blocks to carry the athlete through competition day.

The Engine of Block Periodization: Residual Training Effects (RTE)

The entire ATR model functions successfully because of Residual Training Effects—the retention of physiological changes after the cessation of targeted training. Block periodization exploits these timelines: basic abilities with long residuals are trained first (Accumulation), while specific abilities with short residuals are trained right before competition (Realization).

Why it matters for Endurance: Aerobic endurance and max strength adaptations last ~30 days. This allows endurance athletes to completely shift away from high-volume base training during Transmutation/Realization, knowing their aerobic foundation will survive until race day.
Why it matters for Power/Speed: Alactic speed and maximal power dissipate rapidly (within 5-8 days). Therefore, the Realization block for sprinters/power athletes must perfectly time neuromuscular peaking right up to the day of competition, utilizing high-velocity, low-volume loads.
