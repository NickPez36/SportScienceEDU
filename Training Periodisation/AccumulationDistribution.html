Tactical Periodization:
The Accumulation-Distribution Model

Physical fitness is useless if the athlete is too neurologically fatigued to execute tactics. This model explores how to distribute 'Stress Days' to ensure athletes arrive at competition physically prepared and cognitively fresh.

The "Freshness" Imperative

In the Accumulation phase, we build the engine through concentrated loading—fatigue is expected and tactical execution suffers.

During the Distribution phase (the competitive microcycle), the goal shifts. We must distribute physical stress strategically throughout the week. The objective is to maintain physiological adaptations while shedding central nervous system (CNS) fatigue, ensuring the athlete is "fresh" to absorb complex tactical information and execute highly technical movements on game day.

The Endurance Athlete

Marathon, Cycling, Triathlon

During the distribution phase leading to an event, volume (long-slow distance) is reduced to shed systemic fatigue. Stress days focus on high-intensity intervals to maintain V02 max and neuromuscular tension without creating deep metabolic exhaustion.

Staggering Strategy

Intervals (High CNS/Metabolic Stress) are placed early/mid-week. Long-slow recovery acts as active clearance. The days immediately preceding the event demand high tactical readiness for pacing strategies, requiring minimal preceding physical stress.
