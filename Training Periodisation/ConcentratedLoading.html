Planned Functional Overreaching (FOR)

A strategic physiological manipulation designed to induce a temporary decrement in performance capacity, followed by a targeted taper. This process forces profound systemic adaptation, resulting in a supercompensatory "rebound" peak. Success relies on precise manipulation of load parameters specific to the athlete's metabolic profile.

Endocrine Markers: The C:T Ratio

The primary biochemical indicator of training stress vs. adaptation is the Cortisol to Testosterone (C:T) ratio. Cortisol (C) represents catabolic, stress-driven tissue breakdown, while Testosterone (T) drives anabolic recovery. During a planned FOR block, we intentionally drive the C:T ratio upward (increasing catabolism). The subsequent taper allows Testosterone to surge while Cortisol clears, driving the ratio down and triggering the supercompensation rebound.

Phase 1: Accumulation

Load increases. C:T ratio remains stable as the body copes with normal stress.

Phase 2: Overreach (FOR)

Intentional overload. Cortisol spikes, Testosterone drops. C:T ratio elevates significantly (>30% baseline change indicates deep FOR).

Phase 3: Taper & Rebound

Load drops. Cortisol plummets. Testosterone rebounds above baseline. Performance peaks as fatigue dissipates.

Risk of Non-Functional Overreaching (NFOR)

If the overreach block is too severe or the taper too short, the athlete enters NFOR (characterized by long-term performance decrements, illness, or injury). The specific physiological systems that fail first differ fundamentally based on the metabolic profile being trained. Understanding these failure points is critical for avoiding burnout.

Endurance Profile Risks

Primary risk is Metabolic Fatigue & Tissue Degradation. Prolonged volume overreach deeply depletes muscle glycogen, leading to parasympathetic exhaustion. Bone stress injuries and chronic tendinopathies are the physical manifestations of NFOR here.

Mid-Distance Profile Risks

Primary risk is Endocrine Disruption & Acidosis. High-intensity overreaching (near VO2 max/high lactate) puts immense stress on the buffer systems and adrenal glands. NFOR presents as sympathetic hyperactivity (elevated resting heart rate, sleep disruption) and a crash in C:T ratio.

Power Profile Risks

Primary risk is CNS Fatigue. Density-based overreaching (heavy loads, short rest) demands extreme neuromuscular drive. The peripheral muscle may be fine, but the Central Nervous System loses the ability to recruit high-threshold motor units. NFOR presents as a sudden, inexplicable drop in peak force generation and demotivation.
