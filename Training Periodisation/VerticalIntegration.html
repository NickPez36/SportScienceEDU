Mastering the CNS:
Vertical Integration & High-Low

Pioneered by legendary sprint coach Charlie Francis, this methodology revolutionizes athletic programming. It discards traditional "block" periodization in favor of maintaining all bio-motor qualities year-round, while strictly polarizing Central Nervous System (CNS) strain to optimize recovery and peak performance.

Explore the Theory View Athlete Stacks

1. Vertical Integration

Traditional block periodization develops one quality (e.g., endurance) then drops it to build another (e.g., speed), leading to detraining of the neglected quality. Vertical Integration ensures all elements (Speed, Strength, Endurance) remain in the program vertically. Only the volume and focus change throughout the macrocycle.

Quality Progression over a Season

Continuous Stimulus

A sprinter never stops sprinting fast. An endurance athlete never stops touching speed. The intensity remains specific to the sport year-round.

Fluctuating Volume

While all elements are present, they are not emphasized equally. Volume shifts based on the phase (GPP, SPP, Competition).

Interaction Effect

Maintaining a baseline of strength aids in the development of speed. Maintaining a baseline of aerobic capacity aids in recovery between high-intensity efforts.

2. The High-Low CNS Approach

The Central Nervous System dictates power output. The High-Low system states that training days should either be highly demanding on the CNS (Max Speed, Heavy Weights) or strictly low demand (Tempo, Active Recovery). The "Medium" zone is the black hole of training: it creates too much fatigue to recover from quickly, but doesn't provide enough stimulus to increase maximum capacity.

High Days

>95% Intensity

Goal: Push the absolute ceiling of the athlete's capacity.

  • Maximal Velocity Sprinting
  • Maximal Strength (Heavy Weights, < 5 reps)
  • Intense Plyometrics / Shock Method
  • Requires 48-72 hours of CNS recovery.

Low Days

< 75% Intensity

Goal: Facilitate recovery, build capillary density, and flush the system.

  • Extensive Tempo (Sub-maximal aerobic intervals)
  • Mobility, Yoga, Pool sessions
  • General Strength circuits (Bodyweight/Med balls)
  • Leaves the CNS fresh for the next High Day.

CNS Intensity Distribution

Notice the avoidance of the 76%-94% "Medium" zone, preventing CNS exhaustion.

Comparative Training Stacks

While the High-Low framework is universal, the definition of what constitutes a "High" stressor changes depending on the bio-motor demands of the sport. Select an athlete profile below to see how Vertical Integration and High-Low are applied in practice.

Power Athlete (Sprinter)

Focuses on absolute maximal output, alactic energy systems, and extreme fast-twitch fiber recruitment. The highest CNS demand of any athlete profile.

Defining the "High" Intensity

> 95% of Max Velocity (Speed) & > 90% 1RM (Strength)

For a sprinter, anything below 95% speed alters mechanics and doesn't stimulate the highest threshold motor units. It requires full systemic recovery between reps.

High Day Stack 🔥
  • • Alactic short sprints (10m-60m)
  • • Max Plyometrics (Depth Jumps)
  • • Max Strength (Squat, Cleans)
  • • Complete rest between reps (3-5 min)
Low Day Stack 🔋
  • • Extensive Tempo (100m @ 65-75%)
  • • Core & Med Ball Circuits
  • • Dynamic Mobility
  • • Active recovery modalities
