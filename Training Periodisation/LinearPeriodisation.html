Traditional Linear Periodization

A technical analysis of the classic macrocycle structure. This section outlines the foundational principle: the inverse relationship between training volume and training intensity as an athlete progresses toward competition.

The Matveyev Macrocycle Model

▮ General Preparatory Phase (GPP)

High volume, low intensity. Focus on structural changes, tissue tolerance, and broad base conditioning. Hypertrophy and basic aerobic capacity are prioritized.

▮ Specific Preparatory Phase (SPP)

Volume drops, intensity rises. Training mimics the biomechanical and metabolic demands of the sport. Shift from morphological adaptation to neural optimization.

▮ Competition Phase (Peaking)

Volume drops drastically to dissipate fatigue (tapering), while intensity is maintained to keep the Central Nervous System (CNS) primed. Supercompensation occurs.

The Physiological Shift

The transition from GPP to SPP is defined by a shift from altering muscle morphology (Hypertrophy) to enhancing neural drive (Maximal Strength). Explore the distinct adaptations below.

💪

Building the Engine

During the GPP, the primary objective is structural adaptation. By utilizing higher volumes (e.g., 3-5 sets of 8-15 reps at 60-75% 1RM), the athlete increases the cross-sectional area (CSA) of the muscle fibers.

  • ✓ Myofibrillar Hypertrophy: Increase in contractile proteins (actin/myosin).
  • ✓ Connective Tissue: Strengthening of tendons and ligaments to handle future high loads.
  • ✓ Work Capacity: Enhanced ability to tolerate training stress and clear metabolites.

Sport Science Core Insight:

"Hypertrophy increases the theoretical ceiling for force production. A larger muscle has more contractile units, but without the subsequent SPP phase, the athlete cannot fully recruit them effectively in sport-specific movements."

⚡

Tuning the Engine

Entering the SPP, training shifts to high loads and low volumes (e.g., 3-5 sets of 1-5 reps at 85-100% 1RM). The focus moves from the muscle tissue itself to the Central Nervous System (CNS) governing it.

  • ✓ Motor Unit Recruitment: Ability to activate high-threshold, fast-twitch motor units.
  • ✓ Rate Coding: Increasing the frequency of neural impulses to the muscle, generating force faster (RFD).
  • ✓ Inter-muscular Coordination: Optimizing the synergy between agonist and antagonist muscles during movement.

Sport Science Core Insight:

"Maximal strength is a neural phenomenon. By lifting heavy, the CNS 'learns' to access the new muscle tissue built in GPP, converting raw size into usable, sport-specific force and power."

Application Across Phenotypes

Linear periodization is a framework, not a strict recipe. See how the transition from GPP to SPP is manipulated based on the distinct physiological demands of an Endurance Runner, a Middle-Distance Swimmer, and a Track Sprinter.

Endurance Runner (Marathon/10k)

Focuses on maximizing aerobic engine and tissue tolerance to repeated impact. Strength work is supplemental but critical for economy.

GPP Focus

High mileage, low-intensity steady state (Zone 2). Gym work focuses on basic structural balance and core endurance to handle road volume.

SPP Focus

Volume stabilizes, intensity shifts to Lactate Threshold and VO2 Max intervals. Gym work shifts to heavy, low-rep lower body lifts to increase running economy via tendon stiffness, without adding bulk.

Physiological Demand Profile

The Taper Mechanics

The competition phase requires a taper: a purposeful reduction in training load to dissipate fatigue while maintaining fitness. The duration and depth of this drop vary drastically by phenotype due to central vs. peripheral fatigue rates.

Why Phenotypes Taper Differently

  • ▶ Track Sprinter: Generates massive Central Nervous System (CNS) fatigue. Neural recovery is slow. Requires a long taper (14-28 days) with a sharp drop in volume, maintaining very short, maximal intensity bursts.
  • ▶ Mid-Distance Swimmer: Relies heavily on "feel for the water" and localized muscular power endurance. Taper is medium (10-21 days). Dropping volume too fast ruins their kinesthetic feel.
  • ▶ Endurance Runner: Fatigue is largely metabolic and muscular (peripheral). Recovery is relatively quick. A long taper leads to detraining of the aerobic enzymes. Short taper (7-14 days).
