Fuel for the Work Required

A strategic framework for titrating carbohydrate availability ($3\text{--}12\text{ g/kg/day}$) to maximize high-intensity training quality and metabolic adaptation across the macrocycle.

The Glycogen Threshold Hypothesis

The "Fuel for the Work Required" (FFTWR) paradigm moves away from chronic high-carbohydrate diets. Instead, it strategically periods endogenous glycogen and exogenous CHO based on the specific demands of the day's session. The core of this is understanding the Glycogen Threshold.

🔬 Metabolic Adaptation

Training with low glycogen amplifies cellular signaling cascades (e.g., AMPK, p38 MAPK, PGC-1$\alpha$). This forces the body to adapt by increasing mitochondrial density and fat oxidation capacity. However, extreme depletion destroys mechanical output.

💪 Training Quality

Training with high glycogen ensures maximum glycolytic flux, neural drive, and absolute power output. The "Threshold" is the absolute minimum glycogen concentration required to maintain this high-intensity quality while still securing enhanced molecular adaptation.

The S&C Goal: Identify this threshold (~150-250 mmol/kg dw) and manipulate daily CHO ($3\text{--}12\text{ g/kg/day}$) so athletes hit high-intensity sessions just above the threshold, and endurance/recovery sessions below it.

Athlete Profile Analysis

The Marathoner

Marathon training requires extreme aerobic volume. The goal of CHO titration here is to maximize lipid oxidation during the base phase while ensuring vast glycogen stores are available for race day to prevent hitting the wall.

  • ▶ Base (5-7 g/kg): Incorporates 'train-low' sessions. Deliberate glycogen depletion to spike PGC-1$\alpha$ and mitochondrial biogenesis.
  • ▶ Build (7-9 g/kg): Volume and intensity increase. CHO is titrated up to meet the threshold required for high-quality tempo runs.
  • ▶ Comp (10-12 g/kg): Massive carbohydrate supercompensation. The absolute priority is maximizing endogenous glycogen stores prior to the 26.2 miles.

Practical Implementation for S&C

📊

1. Assess the Session

Before prescribing nutrition, classify the session. Is it a neural activation day (sprints)? A metabolic conditioning day (lactic threshold)? Or an aerobic base day? The biological goal dictates the fuel.

⚖️

2. Manage the Weight

Every $1\text{g}$ of stored glycogen binds approximately $3\text{g}$ of water. Over-fueling a 100m sprinter ($10\text{+ g/kg}$) during competition week will increase body mass, destroy power-to-weight ratios, and slow them down.

🎯

3. Hit the Sweet Spot

For 800m runners facing brutal specific prep (e.g., $6 \times 300\text{m}$ at race pace), starting with low glycogen guarantees failure. Acidosis buffering and absolute power require high glycolytic flux. Fuel the work required.
