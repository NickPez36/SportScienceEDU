Fueling the Final Kick:
Dual-Source Carbohydrates

In elite middle-distance and endurance events, the margin between podium and peloton often hinges on late-race power output. This report analyzes the science behind pushing carbohydrate absorption limits—specifically comparing 2:1 and 1:0.8 Glucose-to-Fructose ratios—to preserve endogenous glycogen and maximize power at threshold when it matters most.

1. The Intestinal Bottleneck

To understand dual-source fueling, we must understand the biological limitations of absorption. The human gut utilizes specific transport proteins to move sugars into the bloodstream. Relying on a single carbohydrate source quickly saturates its specific transporter, limiting the total energy available to the muscle and leaving residual carbohydrates in the gut, which can cause severe distress.

2. Ratios Analyzed: Oxidation Rates & Gastric Emptying

By combining glucose (maltodextrin) and fructose, athletes bypass the 60g/hr SGLT1 limit. Historically, a 2:1 ratio (Glucose:Fructose) was standard, achieving ~90g/hr. Emerging research demonstrates that a 1:0.8 ratio allows for up to 120g/hr total carbohydrate oxidation. Crucially, increasing the fructose proportion improves Gastric Emptying (GE) efficiency by maintaining a favorable osmolarity relative to the massive carbohydrate payload, reducing gastrointestinal distress.

Single Source (Glucose)

Attempting >60g/hr results in accumulation in the intestines. This draws water into the gut (osmosis), causing bloating, sloshing, and severe GI distress. Emptying is highly compromised.

Efficiency Score: Poor
2:1 Ratio (Glu:Fru)

The traditional gold standard. Allows safe transport of ~90g/hr. Gastric emptying is generally well-tolerated as both transporters are used, reducing residual osmotically active carbohydrates.

Efficiency Score: Good
1:0.8 Ratio (Glu:Fru)

Optimized for extreme intakes (100-120g/hr). The near-equal ratio maximizes transport efficiency. Surprisingly, despite higher total mass, gastric emptying is maintained or improved versus 2:1 due to rapid absorption dynamics.

Efficiency Score: Excellent

3. The Final 20%: Power at Threshold

The ultimate metric is performance. High exogenous carbohydrate oxidation spares endogenous liver and muscle glycogen early in the race. This "glycogen sparing" effect becomes the defining factor in the final 20% of an event. As single-source fuelers deplete glycogen and experience 'bonking' (forced reduction in intensity), athletes utilizing optimized dual-source ratios can sustain a higher percentage of their threshold power to the finish line.

Key Finding

In a simulated endurance event, moving from single-source to a 1:0.8 dual-source strategy can mitigate late-race power degradation by up to 12%, preserving the ability to surge or sprint in the final kilometers.
