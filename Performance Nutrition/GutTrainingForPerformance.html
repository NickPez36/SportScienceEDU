Breaking the 90 g/hr Barrier

A comprehensive protocol for upregulating SGLT1 and GLUT5 transporters in high-performance endurance athletes to maximize carbohydrate oxidation and minimize gastrointestinal distress.

1. The Transporter Bottleneck

Understanding why athletes hit a "wall" in exogenous carbohydrate absorption requires looking at the intestinal lumen. The body utilizes specific active and facilitated transport proteins to move sugars into the bloodstream. Relying on a single carbohydrate source rapidly saturates its dedicated transporter.

✱ SGLT1 Limitation

The Sodium-Glucose Linked Transporter 1 (SGLT1) handles Glucose and Maltodextrin. In most humans, it saturates at an oxidation rate of approximately 60 g/hr. Pushing beyond this with single-source fuels leads to residual carbohydrates in the gut, causing osmotic shifts and cramping.

✱ GLUT5 Facilitation

Fructose bypasses SGLT1 entirely, utilizing the GLUT5 transporter. This transporter can handle an additional 30 to 45 g/hr. By combining Glucose and Fructose, athletes can bypass the single-transporter bottleneck.

The "Gut Training" Effect

Research indicates that chronic exposure to high dietary carbohydrates and targeted training with dual-source liquids can upregulate the density and activity of SGLT1 and GLUT5 receptors, raising the saturation threshold closer to 120 g/hr.

Absorption Mechanics

As a simple sugar, glucose requires no enzymatic breakdown in the gut. It is actively transported across the intestinal mucosa via SGLT1, a process coupled with sodium transport. This draws water into the bloodstream, aiding hydration. However, its high osmolality limits how concentrated a solution can be before causing gastric emptying delays.

Application

Forms the foundational base of almost all sports drinks. Optimal when restricted to ~60 g/hr.

3. The 10-Week Gut Training Protocol

Gut training requires progressive overload, identical to musculoskeletal training. This 10-week protocol gradually increases exogenous carbohydrate exposure during race-specific intensity sessions, driving physiological adaptation of transporter density and subjective tolerance.
